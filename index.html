Windows desktop application

CCV builds a resume from the job in front of you.

CCV is a personal resume tool for Windows. You keep job postings, profile facts, and finished resumes on your own computer. Google sign-in is optional, and it is used only to copy those export files to your own Google Drive.

Google Drive access When you connect Google Drive, CCV requests one permission: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file. That lets CCV create, read, and update files it created. It does not grant access to the rest of your Drive.

What you do with it

  • Open a job posting and keep the description with the application.
  • Generate a tailored resume from your profile and that description, then export a PDF.
  • Track which roles you have applied to, per profile, on this PC.
  • Optionally sync the export folder for the active profile to Google Drive, OneDrive, or Supabase.

What Google sign-in is for

Drive sync copies the files CCV already wrote for a job: the job description (jd.txt), the resume PDF, resume-info.json, and resume-prompt.txt. CCV also reads the email address and display name of the Google account you sign in with, so Settings can show which account is connected.

There is no CCV account server. The app runs on your PC. Tokens stay on that PC. Files move between your disk and your Google Drive.

Your data

Profile details, resumes, and browser logins you save in CCV stay in the app’s local data unless you turn on a cloud provider or send a prompt to a service you configured, such as OpenRouter or a chat site you sign into. The privacy policy lists each case. Use of the app is covered by the terms of service.
